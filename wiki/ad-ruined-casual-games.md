# Ad-ruined casual games

The factory's richest proven vein for games: **small, beloved game genres whose accessible versions have been ruined by monetization** — ads, account walls, trackers, paywalls, artificial scarcity. The love for the mechanic survives; the clean way to play it doesn't.

Evidence gathered across two new-mode research rounds:

- **Drop7** — "best mobile puzzler ever" (IGN 8.5) — was mangled by Zynga and is trapped/monetized on mobile with no clean offline web version. Built as [chainfall](prototype-chainfall.md).
- **NYT killed Vertex**; a 470+ signature petition asked for it back. The bereaved-fanbase pattern: demand with zero supply. (Backlog idea: Trilluminate.)
- **Mahjong solitaire** web versions described as "an ad delivery service that occasionally lets you play."
- **FreeCell** sites gate games behind mandatory pre-game video ads.
- **Connections/Wordle-family** games impose one-per-day lockouts and account nags — see [daily-puzzle rituals](daily-puzzle-rituals.md). Built as [groupline](prototype-groupline.md).

**Why this vein works:** the mechanic is pre-validated by years of love (no design risk), the gap is emotional and articulable (people write petitions), and a fully-local single-file version is the *maximally clean* counter-positioning — see [local-first privacy](local-first-privacy.md).

**Limit of the vein (for the jaw-dropping bar):** a clean clone of a famous game is *satisfying*, not *surprising* — the OMG is capped at "finally, someone did it right." Higher-wow variants: resurrect a genuinely *lost/obscure* design most people never saw (discovery, not nostalgia), add an honest guarantee no original ever offered ([solver-verified generation](solver-verified-puzzle-generation.md)), or invent a genuinely **new core verb** ([novel-puzzle-verbs](novel-puzzle-verbs.md)).

**A worked contrast — [foldup](prototype-foldup.md) (2026-07-06) took the new-verb route, not the revival route.** foldup is NOT an ad-ruined revival: there's no beloved fold-the-board-onto-itself game to counter-position against, because the verb was never claimed. Its wow (validated 8/10) comes from *novelty* — "the most familiar gesture with paper, unclaimed by games" — precisely the surprise a clean clone can't reach. This is the vein's escape hatch from its own ceiling: when a candidate can't clear "finally done right," check whether it clears "no game ever made this the verb." The two routes are complementary, not competing — foldup still ships as the maximally-clean fully-local single-file build this page prizes; it just earns the OMG from the mechanic instead of the counter-positioning.
