# subscription-sleuth — recurring-charge finder

**App, v1 (price-change detection added 2026-07-06).** Stdlib-only Python CLI that finds recurring charges in an exported bank/card CSV — no bank link, no accounts. Normalizes cryptic merchant strings, clusters a merchant's charges into ONE recurring line (price-change-aware, even when the price steps), detects cadence (weekly/monthly/quarterly/annual) from the median inter-transaction gap, projects annualized run-rate (at the *current* price) and next-due date, and **names the exact date a price stepped up/down, old→new, % delta, and annualized impact**.

**Domain knowledge embodied:**

- **Merchant strings are adversarial**: real bank exports carry alphanumeric transaction ids (Spotify), mid-string `#`-store numbers, and varying prefixes — normalization rules had to be discovered empirically, and two real bugs were found only through realistic synthetic data. A corpus of real merchant-string formats would be genuinely valuable shipped content ([curated content](curated-content-as-product.md)).
- **Cadence detection via median inter-transaction gap** is robust and simple; annualizing from *per-charge* amount (not historical total) is what makes cadences comparable. For a charge whose price *changed*, annualize from the **current (most-recent stable) price**, never the blend `total/count` — a hiked Netflix must read $215.88/yr (12 × $17.99), not an average.
- **A price history is a step function in time, so detect price changes by ordered level-detection, not amount-clustering.** The pre-2026-07 pipeline clustered by `(merchant, amount-within-5%)` in one pass, which *fused* cadence and price into one bucket: a real >5% step (Netflix $15.49 → $17.99) split one subscription into two half-clusters under the same merchant — two lines, two annualized figures, and the old-price half's projected next-due date already **in the past** (a projection that's not just useless but wrong). The fix is a **merchant-first** pass: group by merchant only → establish cadence from the merchant-wide median gap (the gate that keeps Delta's two one-off fares from ever merging) → walk the date-ordered charges segmenting them into stable "levels," where a new level only counts if it is **sustained** for ≥ N charges (a lone anomaly is an outlier, not a hike). Ordered-in-time segmentation (not value-clustering) is what correctly reads a reverting promo $10→$12→$10 as "one level, one anomaly," not two levels. Stays pure/stdlib — no numpy ([pure core, thin shell](pure-core-thin-shell.md)).
- **The three noise cases are the acceptance tests, and the sustain requirement is the single most important guard**: (1) within-5% rounding/tax wobble stays one price; (2) a single anomalous charge (a one-time overcharge, a proration) must NOT mint a permanent change — a new level is real only if ≥ `_LEVEL_SUSTAIN_MIN` (=2) consecutive charges sit at it; (3) a lone *trailing* new-price charge is NOT yet a confirmed change (can't distinguish "hike just started" from "outlier" at n=1) — the safe default is to report nothing yet.
- CSV dialect tolerance matters more than expected: header aliases, BOMs, quoted embedded commas, credit-vs-debit sign conventions all appear in real exports.

**Content payload (real US price hikes):** the sample's headline demo is the **real Netflix Standard $15.49 → $17.99** step (announced Jan 21 2025, +16.1%) — recognizable as true, not an invented $9.99→$11.99. The dossier curates a verified 18-row US price-hike table (Netflix, Spotify $9.99→$10.99→$11.99→$12.99, Hulu, Peacock $7.99→$10.99, Disney+, YouTube Premium, Amazon Prime annual $119→$139, Paramount+, Discovery+ — each with effective date, % step, and source) as the [curated content payload](curated-content-as-product.md) for this slice. Watch-out from authoring: Netflix's real new price ($17.99) value-collides with the sample's Hulu ($17.99/mo) — no clustering collision (different merchant keys) but any regression pin must key on **merchant + date**, not amount.

**Differentiator (refined 2026-07):** Rocket Money *does* already push a "subscription price increase" alert, so the wow is NOT "the SaaS can't detect hikes." It is **transparent, local, exact, re-derivable attribution** — a deterministic table naming the exact transition date, old→new amounts, %, and the +$30.00/yr bite, computed from a CSV you already have with nothing leaving the machine — versus a push notification in a paid app you linked your bank to. Same shape as [settleup](prototype-settleup.md)'s `--breakdown` shipping the itemized transparency Splitwise buries.

**Open direction:** `--json`, per-cluster confidence, free-trial detection ($0 → paid step); softening the trailing-single-charge default with recency weighting (a future heartbeat).
